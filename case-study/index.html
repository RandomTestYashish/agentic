Agentic

Designed an AI layer for the Airtel app that finishes tasks instead of answering questions, cutting a data top-up from 7 steps to 3.

Role
Lead Product Designer, end to end
Team
Product + Engineering + Data
Platform
Mobile app, text and voice
Company
Airtel · 300 Mn users

What are we solving?

People open the Airtel app to do one thing: check data, recharge, pay a bill, fix the internet. The app made them search menus to do it, and many gave up and called customer care. Replacing menus with an AI meant solving 3 challenges:

  1. Navigation: a person with a clear goal still had to find the right entry point and walk several screens for a simple task.
  2. Hand-offs: when the path wasn’t clear, people moved to a call centre or store, and every hand-off raised operating costs.
  3. Trust: an assistant that reads your account and can spend your money has to earn trust before its first answer.

How did we solve it?

Instead of improving the menus, I proposed Agentic: a conversation that understands what people want and does it, inside the chat.

  1. A pill in the header cycles real asks. People type or speak in English or Hinglish, and answers come back as cards with the next action inside.
  2. Recharges, bills and network fixes finish in the conversation. When a fix fails, the complaint arrives pre-filled.
  3. A one-time first run sets the rules, the thinking state names real steps, and money never moves without a confirm.

Key Considerations

The entry sits where intent forms

A pill in the top bar cycles real requests instead of a floating button or a new tab. People decide what they came for at the top of the screen, and a rotating prompt teaches what it can do. It takes the search slot, so search stays reachable inside the conversation.

Agree on the rules before the first answer

A one-screen first run says what it is good at, that it is still learning, and what never to share. Setting expectations early lowers the cost of a wrong answer later and covers consent for account access, for one extra tap shown once.

Show the work while it thinks

The status line names real steps, like “Checking current usage…”. A spinner says “wait”; a named step says “I’m doing what you asked”. Engineering agreed to send real stage events so the steps always match the backend.

Answers are cards, not paragraphs

Anything with numbers renders as a card from the app’s design system, with its action inside. A usage bar reads faster than a sentence, and a button inside the answer turns reading into doing.

Recommend one, and say why

One “Best match” pack with a reason, and two alternatives below. A wall of packs is the old app again; one explained pick reads as advice. The pick follows usage, not margin.

Money never moves without a confirm

Every purchase opens a sheet with amount, item, number and payment method, and Pay is a separate tap. An agent that can spend is only trusted if it always asks.

Diagnose first, escalate with context

“My internet is slow” runs a visible check, explains the cause and lists fixes. Many issues are fixable in a minute; when they aren’t, the ticket arrives pre-filled.

When it can’t help, say what it can do

Out-of-scope asks get an honest “I’m still learning that” and a list of what it can do now. A confident wrong answer loses trust fastest, so it admits limits by design.

The Process

1

Research

I studied ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Perplexity, Arc and Grok on the same tasks. Six patterns kept repeating: guided starters, follow-ups, one accent, inline controls, widgets and trust signals.

2

Principles

You stay in control. Assist when it matters. Be transparent. Fail gracefully: when unsure, ask, suggest or escalate, never bluff.

3

Conversation Model

One Experience Agent owns the conversation, while Buy and Serve agents do the work behind guardrails. Agreeing on this decided half the UI.

4

Design Exploration

The AI’s colour took four rounds (coral, amber, black, then one dot texture lit by the orb) and the loader took six, ending on one rule: it must never look like the assistant.

5

Prototype in Code

A working prototype with streaming replies, cards, payments and voice settled debates about pace and trust that static frames couldn’t.

Outcome

7 → 3steps to add a data top-up, counted by walking both flows
4core tasks finished inside one conversation
2languages, English and Hinglish, by text or voice
  • Data, recharge, bills and network issues all complete in the chat, without opening a menu.
  • Design set the vision and turned a broad AI ambition into a conversation model and a working prototype the team could build from.
  • Success is measured by task completion in the chat, digital-to-assisted hand-offs per 1,000 sessions, fallback recovery and post-chat rating.

What I learned

  • Prototype the feeling early. A working prototype settled debates static frames couldn’t.
  • Design the system, not the screen. Agreeing on the conversation model decided half the UI.
  • Trust is a budget. Surprises spend it; confirms, honest limits and visible work refill it.
